# GPT-5.6 hard-scenario comparison — 2026-08-02

## Question

The earlier benchmark verified many Iris paths but put all three GPT-5.6
deployments close to the ceiling. This experiment asks a narrower question:
can different GPT-5.6 deployments be distinguished when Iris must investigate
conflicting evidence, decide whether to use tools, and give pedagogically useful
guidance without taking over the student's task?

This is a comparison of deployed Iris behavior, not a claim about each model's
general intelligence outside Iris.

## Design

- 11 different advanced situations, with no support-level rewrites of the same
  request.
- Three fresh candidate trials for every model and situation: 99 candidate
  trials in total.
- The real Iris pipelines, prompts, tools, fixtures, and callback artifacts.
- Exact deployments `gpt-5.6-luna`, `gpt-5.6-terra`, and `gpt-5.6-sol`.
- Reasoning effort and reasoning mode omitted for all three models, allowing the
  provider to select its default.
- No changes to Iris product behavior or prompts.

There is no reference-answer string or similarity threshold. Each situation has
human-readable behavioral criteria rated as achieved, partly achieved, or not
achieved by the shared GPT-5.4 evaluator. Explicit critical incidents are
reported separately. IrisScore is the equal-weight mean of the criteria.

The three tutor situations are the main new stress test. They use realistic
multi-file repositories, build or submission evidence, and histories with
plausible distractions:

- `tutor-workbook-investigation`: separate cache identity, dependency
  invalidation, and retained evaluation state.
- `tutor-stream-recovery-investigation`: separate checkpoint alignment, keyed
  state redistribution, and idle-input watermark handling despite a serializer
  distraction.
- `tutor-hm-regeneration`: recover from an incomplete prior hint and separate
  fresh scheme instantiation, substitution composition, and infinite-type
  detection.

The other eight situations cover course planning with conflicting records,
lecture reasoning from supplied material, and critique of ambiguous written
work.

| Model           | IrisScore | Critical-incident trials | Trials with an Iris activity | Mean / maximum repeat span |
| --------------- | --------: | -----------------------: | ---------------------------: | -------------------------: |
| `gpt-5.6-luna`  | **83.94** |            3 / 33 (9.1%) |              15 / 33 (45.5%) |               5.45 / 10.00 |
| `gpt-5.6-terra` | **86.97** |            0 / 33 (0.0%) |              17 / 33 (51.5%) |               8.18 / 30.00 |

## Paired uncertainty

The following comparison treats the 11 situation means as paired observations.
The interval is a descriptive 95% Student-t interval with 10 degrees of freedom;
it is not corrected for multiple comparisons.

| Difference       | Mean points |    95% interval | Wins / ties / losses |
| ---------------- | ----------: | --------------: | -------------------: |
| Terra minus Luna |       +3.03 |  -0.91 to +6.97 |            6 / 3 / 2 |
| Sol minus Terra  |       +3.33 |  +0.17 to +6.50 |            5 / 6 / 0 |
| Sol minus Luna   |       +6.36 | +1.21 to +11.51 |            7 / 4 / 0 |

This supports Sol as the strongest deployment on this hard slice. The
Terra-versus-Luna ordering remains less certain because its interval includes
zero. The Sol-versus-Terra interval only narrowly excludes zero, so the exact
gap should not be overinterpreted.

## What produced the separation

All three deployments retrieved the relevant course records reliably. The
largest behavioral difference appeared in the tutor pipeline:

- Sol inspected repository or exercise evidence in 6 of 9 tutor trials.
- Terra did so in 1 of 9 tutor trials.
- Luna did so in 0 of 9 tutor trials.

Sol used tools in every workbook and type-inference tutor trial. That extra
investigation translated into better separation of independent failure causes
and better recovery from incomplete prior hints.

None of the deployments inspected tools in the stream-recovery situation. All
three anchored too strongly on the serializer distraction and missed important
checkpoint, redistribution, or watermark evidence. That low-scoring situation
is not a synthetic tie-breaker: it exposes a common Iris weakness worth retaining
in the corpus.

The critical incidents also came from that situation. Luna treated
serialization as the sole cause in all three trials; Sol did so once; Terra did
not make that critical mistake but still covered too little of the evidence.

## Evaluator stability check

The saved outputs from the two repeated tutor and course batches were judged a
second time. This covered 42 fixed candidate answers; the candidate models were
not invoked again.

| Model           | Original mean on the seven checked situations | Alternate evaluation | Change |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------: | -------------------: | -----: |
| `gpt-5.6-luna`  |                                         77.62 |                77.14 |  -0.48 |
| `gpt-5.6-terra` |                                         80.95 |                80.95 |   0.00 |
| `gpt-5.6-sol`   |                                         86.19 |                86.67 |  +0.48 |

The ordering is unchanged and no aggregate moves by more than half a point.
This makes evaluator variation an unlikely explanation for the observed Sol
advantage.

## Correction from the preceding experiment

The earlier type-inference experiment initially appeared to separate the three
models, but the evaluator was seeing only the first 4,000 characters of long
answers. After the harness preserved the complete answer, all three deployments
scored 100 on that direct diagnosis situation. Those original numbers are
invalid and are not used here. The correction is commit `bc54f1823`.

This is why the v5 tutor cases ask for investigation choices and pedagogical
guidance rather than another direct request to diagnose every defect in a
repository.

The safety accounting also retains $4.67775000 in conservative upper-bound

No further paid calls were made after this comparison.

## Decision

The hard slice is now suitable for a benchmark leaderboard point estimate: it
has different underlying situations, equal replication, a meaningful agentic
behavior difference, and a stable ordering under an alternate evaluation pass.
It supports Sol over Luna and gives early evidence for Sol over Terra within
Iris.

It does not establish a universal intelligence ranking, and it does not yet
resolve Terra versus Luna confidently. Future corpus maintenance should replace
remaining ceiling cases with independently designed investigation tasks rather
than add more copies of the current situations or tune criteria to preserve
this observed ordering. The complete maintained corpus remains fixed at 50
situations.

Raw candidate outputs and machine-readable reports remain under the ignored
`qa-results/hard-v5-*` directories and are not committed.
